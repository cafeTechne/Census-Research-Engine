# Round 98 example: health insurance at age 65, Michigan PUMS 2019-2023 (exploratory)

Author self-review, not independent; not a registered study. 245,627 persons aged 45-84; weights and SDR replicates; robust bias-corrected local quadratic jumps (percentage points; fuzzy: points of coverage per point of Medicare).

| Estimate | Jump | 90% CI | Bandwidth (years) | Validated |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Medicare coverage (first stage) | +57.8 | +53.5 to +62.0 | 4.0 | yes |
| Any coverage | +3.0 | +1.4 to +4.7 | 5.1 | yes |
| Any coverage per Medicare (fuzzy) | 0.049 | 0.023 to 0.075 | 5.1 | yes |

Checks: female share jump -3.8 points (-9.3 to +1.6); placebo cutoffs for any coverage: age 60: -1.2 (-2.9 to +0.5); age 70: +0.5 (-0.1 to +1.0). Density test (exploratory): p = 0.65.

Card, Dobkin and Maestas (2008) found coverage rising by about 7-10 points at 65 in the NHIS and CPS; ACS coverage before 65 is higher today (post-ACA), so a smaller jump is expected.
